\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Spark configuration tuning.} Automatic tuning of Spark is a mature field. Approaches
include machine-learned performance models \cite{nguyen2018tuning}, tuning from a single
profiling run \cite{buchaca2020yoro}, low-overhead online tuning of Spark SQL
\cite{xin2022locat}, general online tuning under multiple goals and constraints
\cite{li2023online}, fine-grained stage-level tuning integrated with adaptive query execution
\cite{lyu2024optimizer}, and explicit trade-offs between tuning overhead and flexibility
\cite{li2025loftune}. The survey by Herodotou \emph{et al.}~\cite{herodotou2020survey} names
cost-aware, workload-adaptive tuning as an open direction. In this line of work the objective
is execution performance, and the cost of tuning is something to reduce, not a quantity to
weigh against the number of future executions.

\textbf{Workload-aware optimization.} Workload characteristics are widely used to guide
optimization: transfer learning across workloads \cite{shen2023rover}, runtime statistics in
adaptive execution for lakehouses \cite{xue2024aqe}, large-scale characterization of cloud
workloads \cite{parayil2025workload}, and learning-based parameter recommendation
\cite{bao2018learning}. These systems use workload features to find a faster configuration
sooner; they do not decide whether optimizing is worthwhile at all.

\textbf{Compaction and small files.} AutoComp \cite{gruenheid2025autocomp} automates compaction
for log-structured tables in production data lakes and explicitly balances its benefit against
its cost, which makes it the closest prior work on compaction economics. Practitioner sources
describe compaction mechanisms and best practice for Iceberg and Delta Lake
\cite{goswami2023iceberg,delta2025compaction} without a controlled evaluation of when compaction
pays off. LST-Bench \cite{camacho2024lstbench} benchmarks log-structured tables over time,
including table maintenance, but measures rather than decides. Recent work applies
reinforcement learning to compaction policy \cite{venkatesan2025autocompactionai} and improves
how small files are merged in HDFS \cite{chettaoui2025merging}.

\textbf{Table and data-layout optimization.} PTO \cite{meduri2026pto} predicts, with learned
models over workload telemetry, whether a layout optimization will help before it is applied,
at lakehouse-catalog scale; it is the closest related work overall. AutoLiquid
\cite{nakandala2026autoliquid} automates data-layout optimization in the Databricks lakehouse,
and LayoutPilot \cite{liu2026layoutpilot} is a physical-design advisor for lakehouse tables.
These systems confirm a move toward workload-driven layout decisions, but none of them states
the decision as a break-even over the future executions of a recurring pipeline.

\textbf{Optimization cost and break-even analysis.} Fekry \emph{et al.}
\cite{fekry2020tune,fekry2020tuneful} show that whether configuration tuning is worthwhile
depends on how often a workload will be executed, and propose amortizing tuning cost over
future runs; this is the principle our study builds on. OptEx \cite{sidhanta2016optex} gives a
closed-form cost and time model for choosing a Spark cluster composition under a deadline, for
a single job rather than for recurring optimization decisions. A recent preprint
\cite{chang2026crosslayer}, not yet peer-reviewed, applies a measurement-driven break-even point
to hardware-offload decisions in database systems, which suggests that the mechanism transfers
across domains.

\textbf{Cost-aware cloud resource selection.} CherryPick \cite{alipourfard2017cherrypick},
Micky \cite{hsu2018micky}, PARIS \cite{yadwadkar2017paris} and Smartpick
\cite{mohapatra2023smartpick} select cloud instances, virtual machines or serverless versus
VM deployments for analytics workloads. They decide which \emph{resources} to provision; we
decide whether to apply a \emph{pipeline-level optimization} to an already provisioned Spark
pipeline. The two decisions are complementary.

\textbf{Positioning.} Table~\ref{tab:positioning} summarizes how this study differs from the
closest work. In short, prior systems automate one optimization category, often with learned
predictors and at production scale; we evaluate, in a controlled setting, a single explicit
break-even rule across an execution-level action and a storage-level action.
