\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{Internal validity.} The laptop CPU mixes fast and slow cores, which caused occasional
slow runs: 5 of 32 A1 cells, 6 of 18 A2 cells and 2 of 40 validation cells had CV above 0.2.
We used medians, randomized blocks and warm-up runs, and removing the slowest run of each noisy
A1 cell did not change the conclusions. In A2, warm-up runs left the files in the operating
system's cache, so the compaction benefit we report is a lower bound for cold reads. The A1
study runs read cached input, while the validation runs read from disk; the tuning estimates
still transferred within 3.4 points. Validation used only 4 workloads and 3 blocks, so its
confidence intervals are wide.

\textbf{Construct validity.} All costs are \emph{estimated} from one published price and two
cost measures; nothing was billed. The price does not affect $\Nstar$, but real bills include
storage, start-up and minimum charges that we do not model. We charge tuning with a full search
over 8 settings; a smarter search would cost less, and we report the no-search case as a lower
bound. Adaptive query execution, which can merge shuffle partitions automatically, was turned
off to isolate the effect of the setting \cite{spark2024tuning}. We applied A1 and A2 separately;
because the A2 benefit partly depends on the 200-partition default, the gain from compaction
\emph{after} tuning may be smaller.

\textbf{External validity.} All runs used one Windows laptop in Spark local mode, with no
network shuffle and no cloud storage. Windows' slow creation of small files probably makes the
200-partition penalty larger than on Linux or cloud object stores; we expect the effect to be
smaller there, though not reversed. The data are synthetic, at most 28\,MB on disk, and the
study workloads use one seed; on larger data the best partition count will be higher. The
nearest-neighbour estimate cannot extrapolate beyond the measured workloads (all leave-one-out
misses were at the edge of the range) and, under P1, over-estimated the wall-clock gain of
compaction on two unseen workloads: it cut their task time but hardly their wall-clock time,
and our three features do not explain why.
